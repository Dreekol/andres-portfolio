## The challenge

Cargill wanted its products around the world to look and behave consistently, without every team solving the same problems on its own. A design system existed, but a system only helps if teams know it, trust it and actually build with it.

<Step n={1}>Audit</Step>

I started by auditing the legacy design system, the product teams using it, and the templates they worked from. That gave a clear picture of how teams were really using the system.

<Step n={2}>Document</Step>

The system is built on shared foundations: a color theme with alternates, one typeface across a full type scale, and a single icon library. On top of those sit the components. I documented them so a designer could open a component and find what they needed: its anatomy, spacing, variants, states and when to use each one. I also onboarded and trained new team members on the system.

<Step n={3}>Bring people together</Step>

A system lives through the people who use it. I created global ceremonies and ran town halls to showcase products built with the design system. Alongside them, office hours gave designers a place to bring questions.

I worked directly with the designers designing with the system. The showcases and office hours helped them connect, help each other and get inspired by what other teams were shipping.

<Step n={4}>Connect with development</Step>

The same sessions brought designers closer to developers, so they could solve problems together. We co-created a QA relationship and a way to negotiate feasibility, and aligned on ways of working for handoff and design QA, so designs translated into code as intended.

<Step n={5}>Extend to data</Step>

With the global IT group, I designed role-based Power BI dashboards, so every level of stakeholder could find, understand and report on what they needed. I checked in with developers often to keep the designs buildable in Power BI. Then I documented my design choices against chart-usage best practice and added components so future reports stay consistent.

## Result

Cargill teams got a shared system to build on and documentation that answers their questions. Its designers became a community that helps each other through showcases and office hours, and handoff and design QA with developers got clearer. The system also gained standards for data and reporting.
