## The product

Aquí + Frescos is Cargill's food shopping brand in Central America. People buy chicken, dairy, meat and groceries from Cargill's own brands in one place, either at neighborhood stores or through a mobile app with home delivery.

<p style="margin-top: 36px;"><Image src={logo} alt="Aquí + Frescos logo" width={240} densities={[1, 2]} style="display: block; width: 240px; height: auto; margin-inline: auto;" /></p>

## The problem

New customers had to create an account before they could order, and sign-up and login were where they got stuck. The team needed a clear picture of the current experience before redesigning it, and a way for design to work with their product and development teams.

<Step n={1}>Map</Step>

I mapped the registration flow end to end: every screen and decision a new customer goes through before they can shop. I also mapped the order workflow and the customer’s journey.

<Figure src={reg} alt="Registration flow diagram with details blurred" caption="The existing registration flow, mapped." />

<Step n={2}>Assess</Step>

I assessed the experience and grouped what I found by area, from login and password reset to the activation email. Then I turned the findings into ways to take the pain out of registration.

<Pair a={findings} aAlt="Findings slide grouped by area, details blurred" b={opps} bAlt="Opportunities slide: ways to reduce the pain during registration, details blurred" caption="Findings by area, then opportunities for registration." />

<Step n={3}>Set the cadence</Step>

I set up how design would work with the team. I defined a work cadence that takes a ticket through research, concepts, team validation, prototyping, stakeholder validation and dev handoff with QA. I also organized the Figma file by stage, so everyone could see what was in exploration, up for review, waiting on approval or ready for development.

<Pair a={ways} aAlt="Ways of working slide: the work cadence from project onboarding to development" b={deliv} bAlt="Deliverables slide: Figma file stages and a typical timeline" caption="The work cadence, and how the design file was organized." />

<Step n={4}>Redesign</Step>

I redesigned sign-up and sign-in end to end. People can look around as a guest, choose their city first so they see local prices, and sign up with a social account or an email. Signing back in got the same care: clear help when a login fails, a simple way to reset a password, and Touch ID so returning customers can skip typing.

<Figure src={signup} alt="Four sign-up screens: welcome with a guest option, choose your city, sign-up options, and create a password with clear rules" caption="Getting started: look around as a guest, choose a city, then sign up your way." />

<Figure src={signup2} alt="Four sign-up screens: city confirmed, confirm a phone number, create a password with clear rules, and account confirmed" caption="Signing up: confirm a city and a phone number, set a password, and you're in." />

<Figure src={signin} alt="Four sign-in screens: help after failed logins, choose how to reset a password, turn on Touch ID, and Touch ID added" caption="Signing back in: password help and Touch ID." />

<Step n={5}>Look ahead</Step>

Past those first pains, I laid out concepts for the home screen, search, checkout and the customer profile. They gave the team a starting point for each future feature, ready to place on the roadmap.

<Figure src={concepts} alt="Four concept screens shown as wireframes: home, search, choosing delivery or pick-up, and the customer profile" caption="Concepts for the next features, shown as wireframes." />

<Pair a={future} aAlt="Future enhancement prioritization slide, descriptions blurred" b={next} bAlt="Next steps slide, timings blurred" caption="Enhancements in priority order, and the next steps." />

## Result

The team got redesigned sign-up and sign-in flows, a working rhythm for design and development, and concepts for the next features ready to place on the roadmap.
