## The challenge

Cargill customers needed one place to manage their business with Cargill online. Getting in was the first hurdle: before myCargill, a new customer usually had to email someone and wait to be set up with an account.

I worked with the product owner, a business analyst and the developers to design the portal's front door: the page customers land on before they sign in, and a way to request access on their own.

## The customer's path

<h3><span class="step">1</span>Land</h3>

The homepage does two jobs at once. Returning customers can sign in straight away from the card on the left. Everyone else sees, in plain words, what myCargill is for, and gets a clear way to request access.

<Figure src={home} alt="myCargill homepage design: a sign-in card on the left and the welcome message on the right, over a photo of a field" caption="The homepage design: sign in on the left, the welcome message on the right." />

Further down the page, a short section explains what customers get: immediate access to documents, self-service and information around the clock. It's paired with photos of the people Cargill works with.

<Figure src={value} alt="myCargill homepage section: a headline, a short paragraph and a Get Access button next to a mosaic of photos and green tiles for document access, self-service and 24/7 information" caption="What customers get, explained before they sign up." />

<h3><span class="step">2</span>Request</h3>

Requesting access used to mean an email thread. I turned it into a short form split into three steps: contact details, business information, then the portal they need and why. A tracker at the top shows where they are and how much is left, so the form never feels longer than it is.

<Figure src={steps} alt="Three request-access screens: contact information, business information, and the portal request with terms and a reCAPTCHA check, each with a step tracker at the top" caption="Three short steps, with a tracker showing progress." />

<h3><span class="step">3</span>Confirm</h3>

The last screen confirms the request was sent and tells customers what to do next: check their email to continue.

<Figure src={sent} alt="Request Sent confirmation screen telling the customer to check their email to continue" caption="A clear confirmation, so customers know what happens next." />

## Live today

myCargill is still live at [mycargill.com](https://www.mycargill.com). The sign-in card beside the welcome message and the step-by-step request are still how customers get in. Since then, the portal has grown to cover Agriculture, Animal Nutrition and Food & Beverage, and the request now starts by asking how the person works with Cargill.

<Pair a={accessType} aAlt="The live myCargill request page asking whether you are a current customer, an employee or a new customer" b={portals} bAlt="The live myCargill homepage section listing portals for Agriculture, Animal Nutrition and Food & Beverage" caption="The live site today: choosing an access type, and the portals for each business." />

## Result

Customers can find out what myCargill offers and request access on their own, without an email thread. The front door I designed is still the way in today.
