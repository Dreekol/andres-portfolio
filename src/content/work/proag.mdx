## The problem

ProAg wanted a single, cohesive experience that’s easy and intuitive, and gives it a competitive advantage in crop insurance. Its internal systems and teams had grown apart, so design and development lacked a shared foundation.

## What I did

**Learned the business first.** I studied ProAg’s internal systems and information architecture, and how internal and external stakeholders work with crop insurance.

**Ran planning workshops to decide what to tackle first**, and interviewed stakeholders about how they worked with the existing design system.

**Pitched options, not a single answer.** I presented different design-system approaches so IT and product management could weigh expectations and feasibility before committing.

<Figure src={library} alt="Design system library: developer onboarding, what’s new changelog, foundations and components" caption="The library: Figma onboarding, changelog and monthly updates, foundations and components." />

**Built the operating model, not just the components.** I organized ProAg’s Figma instance and created the documentation, ceremonies, templates and onboarding material for people joining.

<Figure src={cadence} alt="Working cadence diagram: planning, design, spec and document, negotiate, handoff, develop, QA, with revise loops" caption="The working cadence, from planning to QA. Avatars stand in for team members." />

**Gave design and development one vocabulary.** Defining components in shared chunks of information created a language between teams, keeping components and products consistent.

<Figure src={language} alt="Button and icon button specs across two variant themes, with sizing and token annotations" caption="Component specs across variant themes." />

## Result

A consolidated, documented system with a working cadence and onboarding, giving ProAg teams a common foundation for a more streamlined experience.
