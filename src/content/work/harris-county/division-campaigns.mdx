## The challenge

Harris County Public Health is organized into divisions, and each one talks to a different audience. Environmental Public Health works with the restaurants the county regulates. Public Health Preparedness & Response gets residents ready for emergencies like hurricanes. Mosquito & Vector Control works to prevent mosquito-borne disease. Veterinary Public Health cares for the county’s animals.

Each division needed work that reached its own audience, while still reading as one department under the brand I set.

## How I worked with them

I engaged with each division directly. I led an audit of their existing materials, then partnered with them to create campaigns and initiatives, along with the collateral that supported them.

<Step n={1}>Environmental Public Health</Step>

**The Food Safety Summit** is a free, one-day conference for the food establishments Harris County regulates. Restaurants come to learn best health practices and hear from other successful restaurants. For the summit, I designed the identity, the event webpage, e-blasts, the conference collateral and the spatial design of the venue. The 2019 summit, the department’s fifth, was themed “The Future of Food Safety.”

<Figure src={fss} alt="The FSS ’19 identity: the logo with a fork and spoon, the e-blast on a tablet with “The Future of Food Safety,” the date and a video, and the printed program" caption="FSS ’19: the identity, the e-blast and the program." />

<Figure src={venue} alt="The summit ballroom: round tables, blue lighting and large screens showing “The Future of Food Safety”" caption="The identity carried into the venue." />

**Take Out Hunger** is Environmental Public Health’s food-donation program. I created its brand: a hand-cut shopping-bag logo, a set of t-shirts with food characters and puns, and a kit of facial expressions and mouth shapes so the characters could be animated.

<Step n={2}>Preparedness & Response</Step>

**“Unpredictable”** told residents how to prepare for disasters, with one line: “Being prepared removes the unexpected.” The orange strike through “un” carries the idea across billboards, display ads and an audio PSA, all pointing to the department’s website.

<Figure src={prep} alt="The Unpredictable campaign: a Hurricane Season Is Here display ad, a square ad and a billboard of a person with an umbrella standing in floodwater, “un” struck through in orange" caption="“Unpredictable”: display ads and a billboard." />

<Step n={3}>Mosquito & Vector Control</Step>

**“Repel”** warned residents about the coming mosquito season. A mosquito-headed figure in a suit and the word “Allow,” crossed out by a hand-painted “Repel!”, ran on billboards, display ads, an audio PSA and a video PSA shown in movie theaters.

<Figure src={mosquito} alt="The Repel campaign: the video PSA on a movie theater screen, a display ad and a billboard with “Repel!” painted over “Allow” beside a mosquito-headed figure in a suit" caption="“Repel”: in theaters, online and on billboards." />

I also designed the wrap for the division’s **Skeeter School Bus**, which carried its message, “Prevent the bite, day and night,” around the county, with a cartoon mosquito, the hotline and the website on its wrap.

<Figure src={skeeter} alt="A van wrapped in yellow and teal for the Skeeter School Bus, with a cartoon mosquito, “Prevent the Bite, Day & Night,” a phone number and the website" caption="The Skeeter School Bus." />

<Step n={4}>Mobile Health Village</Step>

The department brings its services into neighborhoods through a village of mobile units that set up at community events across the county. I designed wraps that make each unit easy to spot across a parking lot while keeping it part of the Public Health brand. Each is bilingual, in English and Spanish: a **Mobile Market** (Mercado Móvil) covered in hand-drawn produce, a **Virtual Health Response** unit (Respuesta Virtual de Salud) and an **Environmental Public Health** unit.
