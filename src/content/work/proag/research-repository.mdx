Interviews, surveys and evaluations were piling up in separate files. Just as the design system holds the design practice together, a repository keeps research usable after each project ends.

<Figure src={foundation} alt="Illustration: everyone benefits from a research repository, with researchers, sales, support and product teams contributing and drawing from it" caption="The case for a repository." />

<Figure src={history} alt="Timeline of research at ProAg from 2018: data-driven insights, then customer-experience research, then UX research" caption="Where UX research fits in ProAg’s research history." />

**Defined what ProAg needed.** One place where customer-experience and UX research meet, easy to contribute to, with a flexible taxonomy and room to grow.

<Figure src={requirements} alt="Requirements: easy to access and share, flexible taxonomy, self-service onboarding, organized storage, scalable features and cross-study insights" caption="Requirements for ProAg." />

**Compared tools and recommended one.** We narrowed the field to four and recommended UserBit for its balance of storing, analyzing and sharing research.

<Figure src={shortlist} alt="The shortlist: Dovetail, UserBit, EnjoyHQ and Condens" caption="The shortlist." />

<Figure src={recommendation} alt="Recommendation slide: UserBit, with a diagram of how the tools overlap across storing, analyzing and conducting research" caption="The recommendation." />

**Showed it with real findings.** We loaded ProAg’s own research to demonstrate tagging and survey analysis.

<Figure src={tags} alt="Repository tag boards grouping findings by frustrations, dashboard focus and confidence navigating systems" caption="Insights surfaced with tags." />
