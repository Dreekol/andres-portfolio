## The problem

Crop insurance agents choose which insurer to place business with largely on how easy its systems are to use. ProAg’s tools had multiple entry points, were hard to navigate and took too long to use, and its product vision called for a single, cohesive experience that would give it an edge. Our SDG team ran a two-phase UX strategy project to find out where to start.

## Phase 1: discovery

**Listened first.** We interviewed 23 people: 14 ProAg stakeholders across 12 roles, and 9 agents from every region. We affinity-mapped the recordings into findings. Agents liked ProAg’s people and service, not its systems: fragmented tools, clunky printouts and a quoter that overwhelms.

<Figure src={interviews} alt="Interview breakdown: 14 stakeholders and 9 agents, with roles, regions and how many other insurers each agent works with" caption="Who we spoke to." />

**Toured the ecosystem.** System demos of five core tools gave us the context around the quoter, which was the main focus.

**Ideated together.** In “How might we” workshops with ProAg, small teams generated ideas against three prompts, then plotted the top themes on an impact and effort matrix. Co-concepting sessions turned them into statements of what agents should be able to say about quoting, printouts and moving between tools.

<Figure src={hmw} alt="How Might We outcome: an impact versus effort matrix of idea themes, with the high-impact, low-effort themes listed" caption="High impact, low effort first: consistent navigation, fewer entry points, clearer printouts." />

<Figure src={coconcept} alt="Co-concepting board: bullseye diagrams of agent statements grouped into quoting, printouts and global seamlessness" caption="Co-concepting, from the agent’s point of view." />

**Framed the choice.** We laid out three strategic paths, from patching tools one by one to consolidating into one platform, and recommended the path that best matched the research and the product vision.

<Figure src={paths} alt="Path to competitive advantage chart: agent satisfaction against importance, with three paths moving from disadvantage to advantage" caption="Three paths, from patching to consolidation." />

## Phase 2: a plan for the plan

**Gave the team a shared picture of the agent.** We wrote an archetype of the average agent, and emailed a ten-question survey to more than 1,000 agents about their front-door experience, navigation and frustrations, which returned 27 usable responses.

<Figure src={survey} alt="Survey outcome charts showing which tools agents use first and how they rate the experience" caption="Survey outcome: how agents get into ProAg’s tools." />

**Concepted a front door.** A dashboard as a home base, focused on the tasks agents asked for and built for people who are constantly interrupted.

<Figure src={dashboard} alt="Wireframe of a dashboard home base with policyholders, updates, news and recent activity" caption="A dashboard as a home base." />

**Found quick wins in printouts.** Without a large redesign, small formatting changes made coverage summaries easier to read and staple.

<Figure src={printout} alt="Annotated printout redesign: branding moved left, previous year shown for comparison and a summary of total premium by crop. Policy and contact details are blurred." caption="A printout improvement. Policy and contact details are blurred." />

**Turned it into a roadmap.** Strategy sessions with leaders across disciplines led to a Now, Next, Later horizon map for product strategy, user research and design.

<Figure src={horizon} alt="ProAg UX Strategy Horizon Map with Now, Next and Later columns" caption="The UX strategy horizon map." />
